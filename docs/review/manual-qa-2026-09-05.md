> Keep the window smaller than the display. An earlier pass used a window taller than the screen,

# 2. Findings

Severity: **Critical** blocks release · **High** ship-blocking for a graded demo · **Medium** noticeable ·
**Low** polish. Status is **FIXED** (verified in a rebuilt player) or **Open**.

## Functional bugs

### B-01 — Drawing accepted touch only; no mouse or pointer fallback — **FIXED**
`StrokeCapture` bound only `Touch.onFingerDown/Move/Up` from EnhancedTouch, and `TouchSimulation`
appeared nowhere in the project. On any non-touch build — the Editor included — menus responded but
no stroke ever reached the recognizer, so the core loop could not be played or playtested at all.
**Severity:** Critical for testing/desktop; not user-facing on Android/iOS.

### B-02 — every "corruption" enemy falls back to one prefab — **Open**

`EnemyPool.ResolvePool` logs `Unknown enemyID '<id>'` and then **silently falls back** to
`_enemyPrefab` (`[Enemy] Soldado`), so an unregistered enemy never fails — it just renders as a
Spanish soldier. That silence is why this survived to a manual pass.

The split is not arbitrary. Every **historical-era** enemy is registered and working — Spanish
(soldado, fraile, capitan, guardia) and Japanese (heitai, kisha, kempei, shokan), plus soldier,
maestro, labo, daan-lihis and the elinquisidor boss. Every **corruption** enemy is not:

    abo-ng-simula, bakod, gapos, hati, iligaw, kadena, mantsa,
    nawalang-mukha, ngatngat, punit, salungat, takip, walang-awa

`4e445288` (31 Aug) gave that family stats and abilities; no prefabs were ever made for it. **None
of the 13 has prefab art** — this is a content gap, not wiring.

| Level | Enemies in waves | Falling back |
|-------|------------------|--------------|
| 1     | 4                | **4 / 4**    |
| 2     | 3                | 1 / 3        |
| 3     | 9                | 6 / 9        |
| 6     | 12               | **11 / 12**  |
| 4, 7–9, 11–14 | —        | 0 — all clean |

Counts: 32 authored `EnemyData_*` assets, 13 registered with a prefab, 19 authored but
unregistered, of which **13 are actually reached by a level**. `sprinter` and `shielded` were also
registered with null prefabs and no authoring data asset — dead rows the pool skipped at startup
with a warning; both have since been removed.

**Severity: High.** Needs 13 prefabs of art, then 13 rows in the EnemyPool Inspector list.
Guarded by `Assets/Tests/Editor/Data/EnemyPoolRegistrationTests.cs`, which pins the exact set of
falling-back ids so the gap cannot grow silently or shrink without being noticed.

### B-03 — Pause menu and character-unlock panel rendered on top of each other — **PARTLY FIXED**
"PAUSED" was bisected by the unlock card while Resume/Restart/Quit drew in front of it. The pause menu
now takes the top of the stack and is fully readable (verified on screen). The underlying flow issue —
two modals being open at once — is a sequencing decision and is left to the team. **Severity: High.**

### B-04 — A stroke the recognizer rejected was reported as a confident character — **FIXED**
A plain straight line scored `GA 0.480` against a `0.60` threshold — a no-match — and the dojo showed a
large red **"GA"**. In a game whose purpose is fixing glyph-to-syllable pairs in memory, that teaches
the wrong pair. **Severity: High.**

### B-05 — "Quit to Menu" went to Level Select — **FIXED (label)**
The button calls `SceneLoader.LeaveToLevelSelect()`, so the behaviour was deliberate and the label was
the wrong half. Relabelled to "Level Select", matching the Defeat screen's button for the same
destination. If Main Menu was the intent (`docs/system/06` says so), change the destination instead —
but that doc is stale on other points too. **Severity: Medium.**

### B-06 — Level 1 applies an empty environment theme — **Open**
`EnvironmentThemeSwapper: Applied theme ''` on every Level 1 start. **Severity: Medium.**

### B-07 — `HeartSystem not registered at wave 0 start` on every level start — **FIXED**
`ProgressManager.OnSceneLoaded` cleared `_cachedHeartSystem` unconditionally. Unity raises
`sceneLoaded` **after** the new scene's Awake/OnEnable pass, so the clear was discarding the
registration the incoming `HeartSystem` had just made, not a stale one. Lifetime is already handled by
`DeregisterHeartSystem`, whose identity check refuses to unseat a newer instance.
Warning count went 1-per-level-start to **0**. **Severity: Medium.**

### B-08 — Missing script reference shipped in a scene — **Open**
`The referenced script (Salinlahi.Debug.ProgressManagerTester) on this Behaviour is missing!` plus a
serialization-layout warning, in the **release** player. **Severity: Medium.**

### B-09 — All 121 recognition templates re-parsed on every level load — **FIXED**
`Singleton<T>.Awake()`'s `return` only exits itself, so a duplicate `RecognitionManager` still ran
`LoadTemplates()` before its deferred `Destroy` landed. `SceneLoader`, `AudioManager` and `EnemyPool`
already guard with `if (Instance != this) return;` — `RecognitionManager` was the one that did not.
Template parse lines on reaching Level 1 went **242 to 121**; "characters loaded" went **2 to 1**.
**Severity: Medium** (mobile load time / battery).

### B-10 — Drawing feedback text never cleared — **FIXED**
`DrawingFeedback.SetMessage` wrote to the label and nothing ever took it down: "Nice — that's the one."
was still sitting over the play area several dialogue beats and an enemy breach later. Transient
messages now clear after a serialized hold (default 3s); the help offer is exempt, because AC2 keeps it
standing for the run of failures. `LastMessage` is deliberately untouched — it is the assertable record
of what the player was told. **Severity: Medium.**

### B-11 — A persistent unstyled panel sits in the upper play area — **Open, now identified**
A translucent dark rectangle is permanently visible below the wave label; the "Wave 1" label's
descenders are clipped by its top edge and enemies pass behind it. A runtime UI probe identified it as
**`HUDCanvas/[Runtime] ActiveCluePanel`** (216x66 at 94% opacity), built in code by
`ActiveCluePresenter.cs:189` — i.e. another **runtime-built placeholder** rather than an authored
surface, the same pattern `docs/system/06` records for the lock notice and restart confirmation.
It is not empty: it carries the active clue text. It just has no authored art.
**Severity: Medium.** (My earlier `FocusModeIndicator` guess was wrong — that one is full-screen and
correctly hidden; focus mode never activated in any session.)

### B-12 — Locked content is silently inert — **Open**
Locked level tiles, locked Almanac cards and Endless Mode do nothing when tapped: no message, no shake,
no sound. A first-time player cannot tell "locked" from "broken". **Severity: Medium.**

## UI / visual issues

### V-01 — Settings panel is entirely unstyled — **Open, High**
Flat dark rectangle, default Unity sliders with hairline tracks, default sans-serif labels, and it does
not cover the screen — the menu logo and buttons stay visible around it with no scrim. Only "Back" is
themed.

### V-02 — "Leave this level?" confirmation is a placeholder — **Open, High**
Flat brown panel, sans-serif text, two flat tan rectangles with grey text as buttons, overlapping the
pause menu underneath.

### V-03 — The Resume button clipped the "PAUSED" title in half — **FIXED**
Numerically confirmed before the fix: `PausedTitle` (600x100 @ y=180) spanned 130-230 while
`ResumeButton` (560x150 @ y=105) spanned 30-180 — a 50-unit overlap. Title moved to y=260, giving 30
units of clearance. Verified on screen. **Severity: High.**

### V-04 — Era Two and Era Three level select is blank — **Open, High**
Both eras show five featureless dark rectangles — no numbers, no scroll frames — and the banner still
reads **"ERA ONE"** on all three eras. Two-thirds of the campaign's level select is unusable. Matches
the known gap that `numberSprite` is assigned for Levels 1-5 only. **The single most visible hole in
the build.**

### V-06 — Default sans-serif font used across most UI — **Open, High**
Pixel font for dialogue, tutorial text, menu buttons and enemy lore; default sans-serif for the Almanac
headers, "Learned 0/17", the Settings panel, learning cards, unlock panels, "Wave 1", "PAUSED", the
Dojo dropdown and the leave-level dialog. The game reads as two products stitched together.

### V-07 — At least four different button styles — **Open, Medium**
Ornate gold plate (main menu, pause, defeat) · cream plate with dark text (Level Select BACK) ·
red-on-white in a bamboo frame (Almanac HOME/ENEMIES) · flat tan rectangles (leave-level) · flat yellow
rectangle ("Magpatuloy") · flat modern red circle (unlock close X).

### V-08 — Enemy name labels are drawn over the enemy sprite — **Open, Medium**
"TUTORIAL" and "HA" render across the creature's face, unreadable. Enemies also sit on a flat
untextured tan rectangle that reads as a missing background sprite.

### V-09 — Endless Mode's disabled state has no button frame — **Open, Medium**
Every other menu button has a gold frame; Endless Mode is bare grey text on the background, so it reads
as a missing asset rather than a locked feature.

### V-10 — Stark white cards on learning and unlock panels — **Open, Medium**
Pure-white rectangles with a thin grey border, holding a smooth purple vector glyph, on pixel-art
parchment. Both the white and the purple are off-palette.

### V-11 — Level Select layout inconsistencies — **Open, Medium**
The "ERA ONE" banner overlaps the top edge of tile 1; the available tile is a slightly different size
from the locked tiles so the grid does not align; the player character overlaps tile 5.

### V-12 — Two different protagonist designs — **Open, Medium**
Level Select shows a smooth, detailed, higher-resolution character; gameplay shows a small dark 32x32
armored sprite. They do not read as the same person.

### V-13 — HUD bled through the Defeat overlay — **FIXED**
"Wave 1", the spent hearts and the pause button painted straight through the 85%-opaque defeat
background. Sibling order could not settle it — the HUD sits under its own Canvas — so `DefeatScreenUI`
now takes the HUD down. Both defeat buttons leave the scene, so the reload restores it.
Verified: the top band is now completely clean. **Severity: Low, but trivially visible.**

### V-14 — Visible background seam — **Open, Low, cause unproven**
A hard horizontal line runs across the play area. The player log reports `Sprite Tiling might not
appear correctly ... not generated with Full Rect` twice, but no tiled sprite exists in `Assets/Prefabs`
or `Assets/_Scenes`, so the source was not located. Both the warning and the seam are real; the link
between them is not established.

### V-15 — Almanac has no scroll affordance — **Open, Medium**
12 of 17 Baybayin cards and 6 of 32 enemy cards are visible, with a clean bottom edge and a button bar
under them. No scrollbar, no fade, no arrow. All items *are* reachable by scrolling — nothing says so.

## UX issues — all Open

These are product decisions, deliberately **not** changed unilaterally.

- **U-01 (High)** Retry replays the whole learning sequence, re-teaching characters the player already
  unlocked in the previous attempt. Verified across two retries. *(Left alone: re-exposure may be
  intentional under the project's spaced-repetition goals.)*
- **U-02 (High)** The opening is almost entirely modal: focus-word panel -> 4 learning cards -> 4
  dialogue lines -> guided tutorial (3 draws) -> up to 4 "NEW CHARACTER UNLOCKED!" panels -> one
  discovery panel per enemy type, each hard-pausing Wave 1.
- **U-03 (Medium)** The first panel says "INA — mother / e/i · na" with no heading and no instruction;
  nothing explains you will draw these to attack until three dialogue lines later.
- **U-04 (Medium)** One character, five names: `e/i`, `i or e`, `"EI"` (two of them on the same panel),
  `O` (tutorial), `OU` / `o or u` (Dojo). The audio file is `O.wav` while the asset is `OU`.
- **U-05 (Medium)** Mixed English and Filipino with no apparent rule ("Magpatuloy", "Got it").
- **U-06 (Low)** Learning cards print the same string twice, in two fonts ("na" / "na").
- **U-07 (Medium)** The cards teach e/i, na, a, ma; the tutorial then says "Draw BA."
- **U-08 (Medium)** No way to pause or quit during the ~10-modal intro.
- **U-09 (Medium)** Level Select shows numbers only — no names, no completion badge.
- **U-10 (Medium)** Dialogue has no "tap to continue" indicator; it reads as frozen.
- **U-11 (Low)** Defeat screen shows no run stats.
- **U-12 (Medium)** Tracing Dojo is bare: no title, no instructions, no start dot, no clear button, and
  your ink vanishes immediately so you cannot compare it to the template.
- **U-13 (Medium)** Recognition margins are thin. Four good-faith BA traces scored BA 0.675 / NA 0.662
  (gap **0.013**), BA 0.905 / WA 0.814, BA 0.734 / NA 0.668, and one trace of the on-screen guide was
  misread as **WA 0.744**. BA/NA/WA sit very close together; worth a tuning pass on real player strokes.

## Audio issues

| Asset | Integrated | True peak | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BGM/01 - Falling Apart (Prologue).wav` | **-8.9 LUFS** | -1.0 dBFS | ~10 dB hotter than a typical music bed |
| `BGM/Weight_of_the_Crown.mp3` | -12.0 LUFS | **+0.4 dBFS** | over full scale — will clip |
| `BGM/homescreen.mp3` | -13.4 LUFS | -0.2 dBFS | |
| `SFX/Buttons/…button-tap-mobile-medium` | **-27.8 LUFS** | -13.8 dBFS | |
| `SFX/Buttons/…ui-exit-menu` | **-10.6 LUFS** | -0.0 dBFS | 8 seconds long for a UI sound |
| `Pronunciation/BA.wav` | -15.5 LUFS | -0.3 dBFS | |

- **A-01 (High) — FIXED** BGM was -8.9 to -13.4 LUFS, ~10 dB hotter than a normal game music bed, with
  a 4.5 dB spread that jumped audibly between scenes, all at `volume = 1.0`. Now all three at
  **~-20 LUFS, spread 0.25 dB**. Repo size is flat (+2 KB across all 14 assets): each MP3 was
  re-encoded once from the original at its own bitrate, so one extra generation and no growth.
- **A-02 (High) — FIXED** Four files true-peaked above 0 dBFS, not the two first reported:
  `Weight_of_the_Crown.mp3` (+0.41), `audiopapkin` impact (+0.32), and the `HA` (+0.07) and `WA` (+0.05)
  pronunciation clips. Now **0 files above 0 dBFS**.
- **A-03 (High) — FIXED** The two UI sounds were 17.2 dB apart. Matched by **peak**, not LUFS: their
  envelopes are too different for gated loudness to mean anything (1.1s tap vs 8s tail — gated LUFS
  called them equal while the exit sound was still 11.6 dB louder on the onset). Now within 0.0 dB on
  peak, 2.3 dB on onset RMS, 3.4 dB on LUFS.
  *Still open:* the exit-menu SFX is **8 seconds long** for a UI sound. Shortening it changes content,
  not level, so it was left alone.
- **A-04 (High) — FIXED** Pronunciation was 6.3 dB apart across seven clips; now **0.20 dB**, at
  -17.5 LUFS. That is only **+2.5 dB** over the -20 LUFS bed and the voice could not go louder without
  limiting, so the separation is now made by **ducking the music under the syllable** (#186): a duck
  multiplier held separate from the fade scale, triggered from `PlayPronunciation`, on unscaled time so
  it recovers behind a paused learning card, cleared on scene load so it can never strand the music
  quiet. Duck level and envelope are serialized; **the defaults are a starting point and want tuning by
  ear**. Note it is unreachable in Level 1 today — see A-05.
- **A-05 (High) — Open** 11 of 18 characters have no pronunciation clip. Present: BA, DA, HA, KA, O,
  SA, WA. Missing: **A, EI, GA, LA, MA, NA, NGA, PA, RA, TA, YA**. Level 1 teaches EI, NA, A and MA —
  **all four silent**, so the first level's audio-learning loop does not exist. No mastering pass can
  fix a file that does not exist.
- **A-06 (Low) — Open** No audio feedback on locked or invalid interactions.

**Untouched on purpose:** the 17 El Inquisidor boss sounds (their 9.4 dB internal spread reads as
intentional design — footsteps at -20, snarls at -12 — and none clip) and the lightning strike
(-13.4 LUFS, peak -1.32, already fine).

# 4. Recommended UI/UX and design improvements

# 6. Overall assessment

**The core is real; the presentation is not finished.**

The thing the project rests on — draw a Baybayin character, the recognizer identifies it, the bound
enemy takes damage — works, and works well. The `$P` recognizer is fast and accurate on honest strokes,
the wave/heart/defeat loop is sound, progression saves, and several screens are genuinely attractive.
The writing is a strength.

What is not finished is everything around that core. Two player-facing screens are unstyled developer
placeholders and a third surface is built from code at runtime. Two-thirds of the level select renders
blank tiles under a banner that always says "ERA ONE". Every enemy in the first level is the same
fallback creature because the prefabs were never authored. The first level teaches four characters and
has pronunciation audio for none of them, in a game whose stated learning theory depends on that audio.
The UI mixes two font families, four button styles and two protagonist designs.

The gap is concentrated in **content authoring and Editor wiring**, not in code. That is encouraging
for a fix schedule — this pass closed eight code and scene defects in a single sitting without breaking
a single one of 997 tests — but the remaining work is a lot of it, and it is the work a player sees.

**Readiness:** demo-able on a touch device by someone who knows to play Level 1 and ignore the rest.
Not ready for release, and not ready for an unguided first-time player.

**Biggest risk to the grade:** a reviewer opening Level Select, paging to Era Two, and seeing five blank
rectangles. It is a five-asset fix and the most visible hole in the build.
